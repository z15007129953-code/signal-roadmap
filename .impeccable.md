## Design Context

### Users and jobs

Signal Roadmap serves product managers, founders, product operations teams,
moderators, and customers using a public feedback portal. Community members
submit, discover, vote on, and discuss feedback. Product teams triage and merge
feedback, make roadmap decisions, and communicate those decisions clearly.

### Personality and emotional goals

The product is **civic, buoyant, and candid**. Community users should feel heard
and oriented. Moderators should feel precise, fast, and in control.

### Aesthetic direction

The primary theme is light and should feel like an editorial community
noticeboard, not a generic SaaS dashboard. Use paper-tinted surfaces,
ink-like text, and a rare persimmon/coral accent. Support dark mode through the
system preference while retaining the paper-and-ink character rather than
introducing neon or glow. Status colors are semantic and must always be paired
with text, shape, or another non-color cue.

### Differentiator

Signal Roadmap should feel like a well-run public town hall: feedback titles and
visible states lead the hierarchy, and decisions are easy to follow. Moderation
views become more operational and compact without losing the shared civic tone.

### Anti-references

Do not use generic identical card grids, cyan/purple gradients, glassmorphism,
glow-on-dark styling, gradient text, thick left or right accent stripes,
excessive pills, dashboard metric-hero templates, or hover-only critical
actions.

### Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support 200% zoom, keyboard and screen-reader operation,
reduced motion, color-vision-safe status communication, and 44px touch targets
where practical.

### Product design principles

1. Feedback titles and status are the primary information hierarchy.
2. Community surfaces are welcoming; moderation surfaces are precise.
3. Color is never the sole carrier of status or meaning.
4. Phone layouts preserve submit, vote, filter, and moderation actions without
   depending on hover.
5. Empty states teach the next useful action.
6. Empty states never invent activity, momentum, or social proof.
